## Right now: food during a power outage

1. **Keep the fridge and freezer closed.** Every time you open the door, cold escapes.
2. **Secure drinking water.** See [Water](wasser.html). Without fluids you last only a few days.
3. **Eat perishable food first:** fresh, chilled, already opened. After that, thawed food, and long-life food last.
4. **Eat long-life food cold.** Tinned food, bread, nuts and dried fruit need no hob.
5. **If in doubt, throw it away.** Do not leave cooked food standing.

## Emergency supplies according to the BBK

The BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance) recommends: you should be able to look after yourself for **ten days if possible**. **Three days** already help a lot. Build up step by step. The recommendation is not an obligation – every household needs its own supplies (allergies, children, pets, storage space).

### How much fluid?

According to the BBK, a person can under some circumstances last three weeks without food, but only four days without fluids. Plan **at least 1.5 litres for drinking** and **0.5 litres for cooking** per person per day. That is two litres. For hygiene, more is needed. Work it out with the [water needs](../tools/wasserbedarf.html) tool.

### What belongs in the supplies?

Mix carbohydrates, protein and fat. An example stock from the Federal Office for Agriculture and Food (BLE) plans with around 2,200 kilocalories per person per day.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Carbohydrates (energy) | Pasta, rice, rolled oats, rusks, crispbread |
| Protein | Tinned fish or meat, pulses, nuts |
| Fat | Oil, nuts, nut butter |
| Fruit and vegetables | Tins, dried fruit, jars |
| Extras | Salt, sugar, spices, coffee or tea, muesli bars |

The BBK names as well suited: dried fruit, tinned food, dried meat and sausage, nuts, rusks and muesli bars. Choose food that is **long-life**, **ready to eat** or **quick to cook**. That way you need little energy for cooking.

### Storage and rotation

- Store **cool, dark, dry and easy to reach**. No corner you cannot reach in an emergency.
- **Rotating stock:** Buy a bit more with each shop, use the supplies in everyday life and buy replacements. This is the **"oldest first"** rule (FIFO: first in, first out). That way nothing goes off.
- **One-off stock:** Buy once and check the best-before dates about once a year on a fixed date. Use up and replace anything that will expire soon.
- Keep a list: [Stock list](../tools/inventar.html). Quantities and calories: [Emergency supplies](../tools/notvorrat.html).

## Cold chain in a power outage

| Appliance | Keeps without power (closed) |
|---|---|
| Fridge | about 4 hours |
| Chest freezer or upright freezer, full | up to about 48 hours |
| Freezer, only half full | shorter – plan cautiously with at most 24 hours |

The 4 and 48 hours come from the US agency FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency). The 24 hours for half-full appliances is **a cautious estimate**. The shorter figure applies.

- A **thermometer** in the fridge shows you the temperature. Above about 4 °C for **longer than two hours**: throw away perishable food.
- Unusual smell, colour or consistency: throw it away. **If in doubt, throw it away.**
- Cool boxes with ice can keep important items cold for longer.
- Do not refreeze thawed meat or fish; cook it through quickly and eat it.

## Cooking without electricity

- Small meals work on a **camping stove**. The BBK also names garden or table barbecues with charcoal or gas.
- **Never in a flat, house, garage or tent!** There is a risk of suffocation and poisoning from carbon monoxide. See [Fire & warmth](feuer-waerme.html).
- Save fuel: lid on the pot, cut ingredients small, cook pasta and rice briefly.
- Much does not need to be cooked at all: bread, tinned food, muesli, nuts.

## Preserving food

This is for advanced users. **Beginners start with drying and fruit.**

- **Drying (dehydrating):** Fruit, vegetables and mushroom slices dry with warmth and air. Without a device, it works on string or a rack in a warm, airy place. They must be **completely dry**. Many moulds still grow at low residual moisture.
- **Canning (in jars):** Fruit and jam are uncritical. **Vegetables and meat are riskier.** Mistakes can lead to botulism, a life-threatening poisoning. According to Wikipedia, the BfR recommends: heat home-canned food again to a core temperature of 100 °C before eating it. Never eat jars with a bulging or leaking lid. A flat lid is **not** proof of safety.
- **Salting and curing:** Plain salting protects only to a limited extent. Curing salt contains nitrite and needs exact amounts. **Do not cure yourself** without reliable instructions.
- **Fermenting (lactic acid fermentation):** Sauerkraut and pickled gherkins are well-known examples. Use only a tested recipe with the exact amount of salt. The vegetables must stay under the brine. If there is mould or a foul smell: throw it away.

## Food safety

The Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) is the federal authority that assesses the safety of food. Without running water the risks rise. So:

- **Wash your hands** before eating and after going to the toilet. Help: [Hygiene](hygiene.html).
- Keep **raw meat and fish** separate from other food and **cook it through**.
- **Keep dishes clean.** Dirty dishes cause diarrhoea.
- **Infants:** do not give honey.
- Do not open or eat bulging or badly dented tins.

## Wild plants: only a few, with caution

:::danger Danger to life: mistaken identity
**If you are not 100 % sure: leave it.** Many poisonous plants look very similar to edible ones. A mistake can be fatal.
- Collect only plants you **know for certain**, and only **in clean places**: not at the roadside, on meadows used by dogs or on sprayed fields.
- Wash everything thoroughly.
- Do not taste **anything “as a test”**. Some poisons only act after hours.
- If you suspect poisoning: **112** or the poison information centre responsible for your area. Write down the number beforehand.
:::

These plants are considered relatively easy to recognise and are described in the literature as wild vegetables. That is **no substitute for experience**: if in doubt, have an experienced person show you a specimen.

| Plant | Edible parts | Important |
|---|---|---|
| Stinging nettle (Brennnessel) | Young shoots and leaves, cooked (like spinach) | Pick with gloves (stinging hairs). Dead-nettle (Taubnessel) looks similar but has no stinging hairs |
| Dandelion (Löwenzahn) | Young leaves, as a salad | Contains white milky sap. Similar species exist (cat's-ear, hawksbeard) – when in doubt, leave it |
| Daisy (Gänseblümchen) | Leaves, buds, flowers raw | Very small and easy to recognise; ideal as a salad addition |

### Do not collect these plants as a beginner

- **Wild garlic (Bärlauch):** Is repeatedly confused with **lily of the valley (Maiglöckchen)**, with the spring leaves of **meadow saffron (Herbstzeitlose)** and with **lords-and-ladies (Gefleckter Aronstab)**. All three are extremely poisonous, and there have been fatal poisonings. The garlic smell when you rub the leaves is one feature; Wikipedia also names the underside of the leaf. Do not rely on a single test. **If you are not experienced, leave wild garlic alone.**
- **Ground elder (Giersch):** Can be confused with poisonous umbellifers such as fool's parsley, cowbane or poison hemlock. **Hands off** if you are not very sure.
- **Berries, roots, seeds:** Do not eat them if you do not know them.

No plant replaces medicine or supplies. In an emergency, wild plants are a **small supplement**, not a solution.

## Mushrooms: do not collect them at all as a beginner

**As a beginner, do not collect mushrooms to eat.** The most common cause of real mushroom poisonings is confusion with edible mushrooms. About nine out of ten fatal mushroom poisonings are caused by death caps (Knollenblätterpilze).

- First symptoms can appear **6 to 72 hours later**. Then there is almost always a danger to life.
- **Emergency number 112** if you have eaten mushrooms and feel ill. Keep leftovers of the meal, trimmings and vomit. A mushroom expert can then identify the species.
- Certified mushroom experts (Pilzsachverständige, PSV) of the German Mycological Society (DGfM) identify mushrooms before you eat them. A list with a map is on the DGfM website (internet only).
