:::danger The most important rules
1. **Wash your hands** with soap if you can: after the toilet, before eating and cooking.
2. **Never put faeces near water.** At least 30 m away from wells, springs and streams.
3. **Keep wounds clean and covered.**
4. **With diarrhoea: drink plenty** and get medical advice early, especially for children.
:::

## Washing hands with little water

Soap and water are best. You need little water for this.

1. Wet your hands. Turn the water off (tilt the jug back or turn the tap off).
2. Soap on both hands. Rub for at least **20 seconds**: palms, backs of hands, fingers, thumbs, fingernails.
3. Rinse with a little clean water. Dry on a clean cloth or in the air.

**Washing station:** Hang a large canister or a jug with a tap in a fixed place, ideally next to the toilet and next to the kitchen. A second person can pour water over your hands. That saves water and you do not touch anything clean while washing.

**Hand sanitiser:** Without water, use a product with **at least 60 % alcohol**. Rub it over all surfaces until your hands are dry (about 20 seconds). It works poorly on visibly dirty or greasy hands. Against noroviruses and diarrhoea pathogens such as *Cryptosporidium*, soap works better. Supervise children so they do not swallow the product.

You can calculate how much water you need for everything in the [water needs tool](../tools/wasserbedarf.html). Making water safe: [Finding & treating water](wasser.html).

## Emergency toilet

If the flush fails, build a toilet without water. The BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance) lists as supplies large, sturdy **bin bags to hang in the toilet**, toilet paper, disinfectant, gloves and a **camping toilet**.

1. Hang a sturdy bin bag in the toilet bowl or in a bucket with a lid.
2. Sprinkle some absorbent material in after each use. For dry toilets, this is for example bark mulch or straw. That keeps it dry and it smells less.
3. Close the full bag tightly. Put it in a sealed container, not inside the flat.
4. Wash your hands afterwards.

**Without any toilet, only in an emergency:** Dig a pit. As a rule of thumb, at least **30 m distance** from wells, springs and bodies of water is given. In permeable soil, such as gravel or limestone, this is not always enough. Dig further away. Cover the pit with soil. This is the exception. Ask your municipality how it will collect bags or waste in a crisis.

## Rubbish and pests

Collect rubbish in bags with a firm seal. Store it outside the flat and far away from water sources. Cover food and do not leave leftovers lying around. That gives rats, mice and flies less reason to come.

## Personal care

A small bowl of clean water is enough for a quick wash: first face, armpits, intimate area, feet, with a flannel. Use **clean, boiled or treated water** when it comes to the face, mouth and wounds. Do not bathe in water that may be contaminated with sewage, floodwater or chemicals.

**Teeth:** Brush with clean water. Keep a toothbrush and toothpaste ready (this is also in the BBK checklist). During a boil-water notice, use boiled or bottled water.

**Menstruation:** Put sanitary pads, tampons or panty liners in your supplies (the BBK list names menstrual hygiene explicitly). Wash your hands before and after changing. Throw used products in the bin bag, not in the toilet.

## Wounds

Cover every wound as it is, with a sterile covering. Wear disposable gloves if they are bleeding. Do not pull out foreign objects yourself. The doctor does that. Heavy bleeding: [First aid](erste-hilfe.html).

- Gently wash small wounds with soap and clean water.
- Cover them with a waterproof plaster or a clean bandage.
- Avoid floodwater, salt water and brackish water with open wounds.
- See a doctor if the area turns red, swells or oozes.
- Even small wounds can transmit tetanus (lockjaw). Check your vaccination record and ask if in doubt.

## Food hygiene

- Wash your hands before cooking and eating.
- Use only safe water for washing and preparing food.
- Keep raw and cooked separate. The BfR (Federal Institute for Risk Assessment) advises separate chopping boards, knives and cloths for raw and cooked food and for anything that will not be heated again.
- If possible, use disposable dishes. Otherwise rinse dishes with hot water.
- Wash cloths and towels often or use kitchen paper.
- Do not eat anything that may have spoiled after a power outage ([Power outage guide](stromausfall.html)).

More about supplies: [Food](nahrung.html).

## Diarrhoea and vomiting

Diarrhoea means: three or more loose stools a day. The biggest risk is the **loss of water and salts**. It hits children, older people and weakened people hardest.

1. **Drink in small sips** and often. Drink safe water (boiled!).
2. **Get an oral rehydration solution from the pharmacy** (electrolyte powder). Follow the pack. The WHO describes it as a mixture of clean water, salt and sugar. The BBK names electrolytes as part of the home medicine cabinet.
3. **Keep eating** as soon as you can. Keep breastfeeding breastfed babies.
4. **Wash your hands often with soap.** This is the best prevention so that the pathogen does not spread in the household.

:::warn See a doctor or call the medical on-call service (116 117) for
- **Blood in the stool**
- Diarrhoea that lasts longer than a few days
- **Fever**, or if **children, infants**, pregnant women or elderly people are affected
- Signs of dehydration: very tired or restless, sunken eyes, strong thirst or unable to drink, skin stays up after being pinched

If the person is unconscious or barely responsive: **112**.
:::
