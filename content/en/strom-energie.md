## Right now: the power is out – what first?

1. **Information:** Switch on a battery or hand-crank radio.
2. **Light:** LED torch or head torch. Do not leave any open flame unattended.
3. **Save the phone:** Power-saving mode, keep a power bank ready (see [Communication & radio](kommunikation.html)).
4. **Switch appliances off or disconnect them from the mains.** When the power returns there can be brief voltage spikes that damage devices.
5. **Keep the fridge and freezer closed.** See [Food](nahrung.html).
6. **Check whether only you are affected:** Check the fuse box and RCD (residual-current device), ask the neighbours. Inform the local grid operator if the outage is not known.

:::danger Generators: danger to life in buildings
- **Run only outdoors.** Exhaust fumes contain carbon monoxide (CO) – invisible, odourless, deadly.
- **Never** in a cellar, garage, flat, tent or carport. Even an open window is not enough.
- Place it **at least about 6 metres** (20 feet) away from windows, doors and attached garages.
- **Never** connect the generator to a socket in the house with a plug. See the section “Never feed into the house wiring”.
- With headache, dizziness or nausea, get into the fresh air immediately. Call **112**.
:::

## Understanding energy: watts, watt-hours, run time

- **Power** is measured in **watts (W)**: how much electricity a device is drawing right now.
- **Energy** is measured in **watt-hours (Wh)**: how much is stored in a battery. 50 watts over one hour is 50 Wh.
- **Calculating:** Wh = Ah × V. Example: a battery with 10 Ah at 12 V has 120 Wh.
- **Run time (hours)** = energy (Wh) ÷ power (W).

**Power bank:** A power bank usually shows mAh. Convert: 20,000 mAh is 20 Ah. At a cell voltage of about 3.7 V that is 74 Wh. According to Wikipedia, this value refers to the battery inside. Less is usable: the electronics raise the voltage to 5 volts and lose energy in doing so. **Calculate roughly with two thirds** (estimate). A smartphone battery holds about 12 to 20 Wh (estimate, see data sheet). The power bank then lasts for about three full charges.

Check it yourself with the tool [Power & battery](../tools/strom.html). Converting units: [Units](../tools/einheiten.html).

### Consumption list (rough guide values)

| Device | Power | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | 1 to 5 W | Very high |
| LED lamp | 3 to 10 W | High |
| Charging a smartphone | 5 to 20 W | High |
| Wi-Fi router | 5 to 15 W | Medium |
| Laptop | 30 to 70 W | Medium |
| Fridge (running) | 50 to 150 W | Medium |
| Kettle, fan heater | 1,000 to 2,200 W | Do not run on emergency power |

These are estimates. The **rating plate** on the device gives the real power.

## Setting priorities

1. **Information** (radio)
2. **Light** (LED)
3. **Communication** (phone, power bank)
4. **Cooling** (keep the appliance closed, do not actively run it)
5. **Medical devices**, if you depend on them. Agree an emergency plan with your doctor **beforehand**.

Heat should **not** be produced with electricity. A battery is not enough for that. See [Fire & warmth](feuer-waerme.html).

## Handling power banks and batteries correctly

- **Charge beforehand:** Charge batteries, spare batteries and power banks regularly, especially when a storm warning is issued.
- **Storage:** Li-ion batteries age faster at a high state of charge and in heat. Check rarely used batteries every 18 to 24 months (according to Wikipedia) and recharge them so they do not become deeply discharged.
- **Heat:** Do not leave them in the sun or in a hot car.
- **Cold:** Cold temporarily reduces the usable performance (estimate). Carry phone and power bank in an inside pocket.
- **Damaged or swollen?** Do not use it any more. Li-ion batteries can catch fire.
- The same **battery types** in the household (AA or AAA) make stocking easier.

## Solar

The BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance) names solar-powered battery chargers and power banks as a help in a power outage.

- **Rated power (Wp):** It applies only under laboratory conditions: 1000 W of light per square metre, 25 °C cell temperature. In Germany the module usually delivers **much less**, especially with clouds, in winter and in shade.
- **Rule-of-thumb value (our own rough calculation, not a measurement):** On average over the year, a module yields roughly 2 to 3 Wh per watt of rated power per day. In winter often under 1 Wh.
- **Orientation:** In Central Europe, facing south with a 30 to 40 degree tilt gives the highest yield. Shading reduces it greatly.
- **Charging connections:** Small modules have USB outputs for phone and power bank. Larger ones deliver 12 V and need a charge controller.
- **Tip:** Charge the power bank with the module during the day. Charge the phone from the power bank in the evening. That way the sun is stored.

### Balcony power plant during a grid outage

:::warn Usually no power during a power outage
A plug-in solar device (balcony power plant) **usually does not work** when the public grid fails. The inverter needs the grid as a timing reference and switches off for safety reasons. This is intended: it protects utility workers from voltage coming from the house.
- Do **not** rewire anything to get around this block. That is life-threatening.
- According to the Verbraucherzentrale, some devices with **battery storage** have an **island mode** and can serve as an emergency power source in a power outage. This is an extra function of individual models. Read the instructions before buying.
:::

## Generator (power unit)

- **Small inverter units** supply clean power for a smartphone, laptop and router. They can supply a fridge-freezer and a few lamps via extension cables (ADAC). Use heavy-duty cables.
- **Larger units** for supplying the house need a **changeover switch** installed by a **qualified electrician**. Anyone who wants to connect a generator permanently informs the grid operator beforehand.
- **Never feed into the house wiring:** No “back-feed” cable from socket to socket.

:::danger Never feed into the house wiring – danger to life
If a generator is connected to the house wiring without a changeover switch, the power can **flow back into the grid**. A transformer works in both directions. The line outside can then be live. This endangers **utility workers, neighbours and rescue workers**. The connection may only be made through a properly installed **changeover switch** by a qualified electrician.
:::

### Fuel and operation

- **Storage:** Petrol and diesel in shatterproof, sealed canisters, well ventilated, away from sources of ignition. Check regularly for ageing. Regulations govern how much you may store at home: ask the fire brigade or your landlord.
- **Let it cool down** before you refuel. Spilled fuel on hot parts can ignite.
- **Keep it dry.** A wet device or wet connected devices can cause electric shocks.
- **CO alarm** with battery on every floor.
- Follow the manufacturer's **instructions**.

## Car battery as emergency power

- **Charging a phone:** With a USB adapter in the 12 V socket. This is simple and sensible.
- **Run the engine** only **outdoors**. Never in a garage or enclosed space. The exhaust must not be blocked by snow.
- **Inverter:** It turns 12 volts of direct current into 230 volts. Small devices (guide value: up to about 150 to 300 W) are enough for a laptop and chargers. Large consumers draw very high currents. Follow the instructions of the inverter.
- **Do not drain it:** A starter battery is built for starting the engine. If you discharge it deeply, the car will no longer start. If necessary, let the engine run briefly outdoors. If a device stays connected for longer, check the remaining charge.

## Light without electricity

- **LED** is the best choice: very economical, cold, no fire.
- **One torch per person**, plus a head torch for free hands. Keep spare batteries.
- **Candles** only on a firm surface, never unattended. Ventilate regularly, even in winter. The Verbraucherzentrale and the BBK advise this.
- **Hand-crank and solar radios** need no batteries. Many have a built-in lamp and a USB output. The charging current is small.
