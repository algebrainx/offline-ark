:::danger Person does not respond and is not breathing normally?
Call **112** (speakerphone on). **Press hard and fast straight away** on the centre of the chest. Do not stop until help takes over or the person breathes normally again. You cannot do anything wrong – except doing nothing.
:::

## First steps in every emergency

1. **Protect yourself:** Look around. Is there a threat from traffic, fire, electricity or gas? Do not put yourself in danger.
2. **Speak to the person:** Speak to them loudly. Gently shake their shoulders.
3. **Get help:** No response? Shout for help and call **112**. Say: Where is it? What happened? How many injured people? Wait for questions and hang up only when the emergency call centre ends the call.
4. **Check breathing:** Gently tilt the head back and lift the chin. Look, listen and feel for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping or rattling is **not** normal breathing.
5. **Breathing normally?** Then put them in the recovery position. **Not normal?** Start resuscitation straight away.

:::info No network, 112 does not work?
Send someone to get help or shout for help. The BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance) advises: if the emergency numbers no longer work, go to the nearest police station or hospital in an emergency if possible. If you are alone and think nobody will come, get help yourself – as fast as possible. When you return, start chest compressions straight away. For light signals, see the [SOS light](../tools/sos.html).
:::

## Unconscious but breathing normally: recovery position

It keeps the airway open. Vomit can drain out. The mouth is the lowest point.

1. Kneel beside the person. Straighten their legs. Place the near arm bent upwards.
2. Take the far arm by the wrist. Place the back of the hand against their cheek. Do not let go of the hand.
3. Bend the far leg at the thigh.
4. Pull the person towards you. Position the upper leg at a right angle.
5. Tilt the neck back slightly. Open the mouth a little. The hand at the cheek holds the head in this position.

After that, keep checking breathing and consciousness. If breathing stops: turn them onto their back and resuscitate.

:::warn After a serious accident
After a fall from a great height or a road accident, the neck may be injured. The guidelines then advise against the recovery position. Turn the person only if it is necessary for breathing, with help, as one unit (head and body in a line). The DRK (German Red Cross) describes the recovery position in general terms. Here, use the more cautious advice.
:::

## Resuscitation (adults)

Applies if the person does not respond and is not breathing normally. If in doubt: press.

1. Kneel beside the person. They lie on their back, ideally on a firm floor.
2. Place the heel of your hand on the centre of the chest, the second hand on top. Keep your arms straight, shoulders directly above.
3. Press down **5 to 6 cm**. Rate: **100 to 120 times per minute**. Let the chest rise fully after every compression.
4. With training: after 30 compressions, give 2 rescue breaths (head back, nose pinched, breathe in over about 1 second until the chest rises). **Without training, or if you do not want to give rescue breaths: just keep pressing continuously.**
5. If an **AED (automated external defibrillator)** is available, have someone fetch it. Switch it on and follow the voice prompts. Keep pressing while doing so, until the device analyses.
6. If there are several of you, take turns about every 2 minutes.

Do not be afraid of broken ribs. Saving a life is more important. The [stopwatch](../tools/zeit.html) can help you keep track of time.

### Children and infants

Call 112 and follow the emergency call centre. For children, press about one third of the depth of the chest; for infants, use two thumbs. Rate as for adults. According to the 2025 guidelines, untrained helpers should first give 5 rescue breaths to children, then 30:2. If you cannot or do not want to give rescue breaths: just press. An AED often has a children's mode. If you are alone without a phone, resuscitate for 1 minute first, then get help.

## Severe bleeding

1. Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth. Keep pressing and do not let up.
2. Lay the person down. Raise injured arms or legs if no fracture is suspected.
3. Call 112.
4. Apply a **pressure bandage**: put a dressing pad on the wound, wrap a gauze bandage around it 2 to 3 times, place a pressure pad on top (e.g. a packaged bandage), wrap over it several times and tape it down.
5. Cover the person and keep them warm (see Shock).

Objects stuck in the wound stay in. Only pad around them.

**A tourniquet is the last resort.** Only for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, when pressure is not enough. Use a tourniquet or a cloth at least 5 cm wide, never wire or shoelaces. Place it between the wound and the heart, never on a joint. Write down the time. The person needs the emergency services immediately.

:::original Show original
"If bleeding continues and all other measures have failed, or if the limb is severed, then apply a tourniquet. Use the tourniquet as a LAST RESORT." (US Army, FM 4-25.11, Section 2-18)
:::

## Shock

Shock means: the body is not getting enough blood. Signs: pale, cold clammy skin, shivering, restlessness, later apathy.

1. Call 112.
2. Lay the person down with the legs raised, if they are conscious.
3. Cover them and reassure them. Stay with them.
4. Unconscious with normal breathing: recovery position. Not breathing: resuscitation.

## Burns and scalds

1. Get the person out of danger. If clothing is on fire: smother the flames with a blanket, put them out with water or roll on the ground.
2. With electricity: switch off the power first, never touch the person directly.
3. **Cool small burns** with cool, not icy, water. No ice.
4. Cover the wound loosely with a sterile dressing or cling film. Keep the person warm.
5. Call 112 for large burns, burns on the face and for children.

Do not open blisters. No ointments or home remedies. **Do not tear off clothing that is stuck.** For scalds, however, take off wet, hot clothing quickly but carefully.

:::warn Experts disagree about cooling
The DRK says at least 20 minutes. Other expert sources say only about 2 to 20 minutes. All warn about hypothermia with **large** areas and in infants and small children. Use the more cautious rule: cool only small areas (according to Wikipedia up to about the area of two A4 sheets), stop as soon as the person feels cold, and do not cool anything large. On the face, use damp cloths only; the airway stays clear.
:::

## Hypothermia

Signs: shivering with cold, later tiredness, muscle stiffness, slow breathing.

1. Call 112. Bring the person to a warm place sheltered from the wind.
2. Carefully take off wet clothing. Wrap them in dry blankets. Also insulate from the cold ground.
3. If conscious: warm, sugary drinks. **No alcohol.**
4. Do not rub, no hot-water bottle.
5. With severe hypothermia (hardly any response): no more attempts to warm them up, handle them gently, cover them, check breathing.

Frostbite (white or bluish skin with no feeling): do not rub, do not move, cover loosely and sterile, do not open blisters.

## Heat stroke and sunstroke

Heat stroke is life-threatening: hot, dry skin, confusion, cramps, vomiting, impaired consciousness.

1. Call 112.
2. Move the person into the shade. Upper body raised. Remove unnecessary clothing.
3. Cool straight away: damp cloths on head, neck and body. Fan air towards them. No ice directly on the skin.
4. Give something to drink only if the person is fully conscious and alert.
5. Unconscious with normal breathing: recovery position.

Sunstroke: headache, dizziness, hot red head, nausea. Out of the sun, upper body raised, cool the head with damp cloths.

## Swallowing and choking

The person cannot speak, cannot breathe or can only cough with a wheezing sound.

1. Tell them to cough.
2. If that does not help: bend them forward. Give up to **5 firm blows between the shoulder blades**.
3. Then up to **5 abdominal thrusts**: stand behind them, fist between the navel and the lower end of the breastbone, other hand around it, pull sharply backwards and upwards.
4. Alternate until the object is out. Call 112.
5. If the person becomes unconscious: resuscitation.

Infants: 5 blows on the back (head lower than the body), then 5 thrusts on the breastbone – no abdominal thrusts. Do not reach blindly into the mouth. Have anyone who received abdominal thrusts examined by a doctor afterwards.

## Broken bone

Do not move, do not set. Immobilise in the position the person finds most comfortable. Cover an open wound sterile. Cool a closed fracture with wet cloths. Watch for shock and call 112.

## Poisoning

1. Protect yourself first. Bring the person out of the danger area.
2. Check consciousness and breathing. Unconscious: 112. Breathing normally: recovery position.
3. Keep packaging, leftovers and vomit safe. This helps the doctors.
4. Call the **poison information centre**. It gives advice by phone around the clock. **Enter the number of your regional poison information centre in your [emergency contacts](../tools/kontakte.html)** before you need it.
5. Do not induce vomiting and do not give home remedies unless someone advises you to.

Contact poisons and gases: do not rescue the person yourself if it puts you at risk. Call 112 and say what you suspect.

## Carbon monoxide (CO)

CO is produced by combustion, especially when oxygen is lacking – for example from a stove, gas heater, barbecue indoors or a generator. It is **invisible and odourless**. Signs: headache, dizziness, nausea, drowsiness. During sleep there is a risk of loss of consciousness.

1. Everyone out into the open air immediately. Before that, open windows and doors if that can be done quickly.
2. Do not stay in the room. Do not bring anyone out if you could become unconscious yourself.
3. Call 112 from outside.
4. Unconscious with normal breathing: recovery position. Not breathing: resuscitation.
5. Even if someone feels better again, they need medical treatment.

Prevention: CO alarm, never use a barbecue or generator indoors. More in the [power outage guide](stromausfall.html).
