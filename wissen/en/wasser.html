Finding & treating water

Where to get water in an emergency and how to make it safe – supplies first, then the house, then outdoors.

Does not replace training. In an emergency, call 112 first, or keep trying. This page deliberately gives no medication doses – ask a doctor or pharmacist.

This does not help

Boiling, filtering or tablets do not make water safe if it contains fuel, chemicals or radioactive substances. In that case use bottled water or another source and ask the authorities. Do not drink seawater either: too much salt draws water out of the body and you die of thirst.

Right now: how to get safe drinking water

  1. Use your supplies first. Bottled water is safest.
  2. No supplies? Get water from inside the house (see below). It is usually cleaner than any source outdoors.
  3. Cloudy water: pour it through a clean cloth, kitchen paper or a coffee filter, or let it settle. Then pour off the clear water.
  4. Boil it at a rolling boil: for 1 minute. From about 2000 m altitude, 3 minutes.
  5. Let it cool down, without ice. Fill it into a clean, covered container.

"Bring clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute (at elevations above 6,500 feet, boil for 3 minutes)." (CDC, How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency)

Finding water

Inside the house (usually better):

  • Water from the hot water tank (the tank connected to your taps). Never water from radiators or the heating circuit.
  • The toilet cistern (not the bowl), if the water is clear and has no cleaning additives or colour tablets in it.
  • Melted ice cubes made from clean water.
  • Liquid from tinned fruit or vegetables.
  • Pool or spa water only for washing and cleaning, not for drinking.

Outdoors – everything must be treated: rainwater, streams, rivers, lakes, ponds, springs. They can be contaminated with animal faeces, sewage and chemicals. In a flood, well water can also be contaminated. If the water has a chemical smell or an oil film: do not use it.

Boiling

The WHO (World Health Organization) says: one rolling boil is enough to kill bacteria, viruses and parasites. Then take the pot off the heat. Let the water cool down by itself, without ice. Protect it from new contamination. Clear cloudy water before boiling. The WHO gives no fixed time. The CDC (US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) recommends 1 minute at a rolling boil, and 3 minutes at high altitude (from about 2000 m). Follow the longer, more cautious figure.

Filters

A water filter must have pores of no more than 0.3 micrometres to stop bacteria, and no more than 1 micrometre for parasites. It usually cannot stop viruses. A household filter, for example in a jug, does not replace boiling. Follow the instructions of the device.

Tablets and drops

Chlorine, chlorine dioxide or iodine products kill viruses and bacteria. Only chlorine dioxide, used correctly, also deals with Cryptosporidium. Read the pack and follow the manufacturer's instructions – on amount and waiting time. Iodine is not suitable for pregnant women, people with thyroid problems or for use beyond a few weeks.

Sun (SODIS)

Fill clear, clean plastic bottles with clear water and lay them flat in the sun: about 6 hours in sun, 2 days when cloudy. Filter cloudy water beforehand. This is an emergency solution. Boil if you can.

What does not help

  • Any water that does not come from a bottle or the public mains must be treated – even if it looks clear.
  • Chemicals or fuel in the water: treatment does nothing, see above.
  • Drinking seawater: your kidneys can only produce urine that is less salty than seawater. You lose more water than you drink.
  • Drinking urine: expert sources advise against it, including the US Army manual (according to Wikipedia).

Storing water

  • Use clean, food-grade containers with a tight lid. Never containers that held chemicals or bleach.
  • Wash them with soap and rinse well.
  • Label them: "Drinking water" and the date. Store in a cool, dark place, far away from petrol and pesticides.
  • Replace water you filled yourself every 6 months.
  • Take water out with a clean vessel. Never reach in with your hands.
  • The BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance) plans with 2 litres per person per day, of which 0.5 litres for cooking. For heat, sick people, pregnant women and pets you need more. More in the emergency supply calculator.

Warning signs

Do not drink if the water is cloudy, smells different, tastes different or has a colour. Tap water should be clear, colourless and free of any fault in smell and taste, says the Umweltbundesamt (Federal Environment Agency). If you become ill after drinking (diarrhoea, vomiting, stomach cramps): Hygiene & sanitation, section Diarrhoea and vomiting.

Boil-water notice: what applies?

If the public health office (Gesundheitsamt) orders measures to avert danger, the water supplier must inform those affected, including about bans and restrictions on use (Drinking Water Ordinance, § 52). A boil-water notice is one of these restrictions. Then:

  1. Boil the water at a rolling boil for 1 minute before you use it for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth or baby food.
  2. This also applies if you filtered it beforehand.
  3. Do not use water or ice from fridge dispensers.
  4. Listen to the radio and announcements from the authorities until the all-clear. If in doubt, ask the public health office.

If the notice says "do not drink" (for example because of chemicals), boiling does not help. Then use bottled water.
