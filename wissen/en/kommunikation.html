Right now: how to stay informed

  1. Switch on the radio. Battery, hand-crank or car radios also work without a socket. Radio stations keep broadcasting when mobile networks and internet fail.
  2. Heed official warnings. Siren sound, loudspeaker announcement, radio message or phone alert: turn on the radio and wait for announcements.
  3. Save phone battery. Turn brightness down, switch on power-saving mode, do not constantly look for news.
  4. Emergency number 112 only in real danger. For fire, a medical emergency or trapped people.
  5. Inform family and neighbours. Agree meeting points (see below).

Warning channels in Germany

Authorities warn through a warning mix (Warnmix): several channels at the same time. If one fails, another helps.

Important: If the mobile network and internet fail, warning apps and phone alerts fail too. The radio stays. Install NINA (the official German warning app) beforehand and check that the emergency alerts on your phone are switched on. Current messages are also on the website warnung.bund.de.

Cell Broadcast is considered robust, even when networks are overloaded. But it does not replace a radio: without a radio cell, nothing arrives here either. According to information on the nationwide warning day (Bundesweiter Warntag), an all-clear is not sent via Cell Broadcast.

Emergency call when the network is disrupted

Emergency number 112
  • 112 is free of charge and valid across Europe.
  • If your phone has no network from your own provider, the emergency call can, according to information on the Euro emergency number, automatically go through another available network. In Germany, a working SIM card is required.
  • This only helps if a network can be reached somewhere. In a total outage no call goes through.
  • If 110 and 112 no longer work, go, on the BBK's recommendation and if possible, to the nearest police station or hospital. Find out in advance where the town hall, fire brigade or emergency meeting points are near you.
  • Briefly tell the dispatcher: Where? What happened? How many people affected? Who is calling? Then wait for questions.

A phone number list on paper helps if your phone stops working. See Contacts & meeting points.

Meeting points and family plan

Plan beforehand, while everything works:

  1. Two meeting points: one right at the house (for example courtyard or lamppost), one in the district (for example school, church, town hall).
  2. One contact person outside the region. Everyone reports to them if they cannot see each other.
  3. Paper list with numbers and addresses of family, neighbours, doctor and emergency contacts.
  4. Arrangements for children: Who picks them up? Where do they go if nobody can be reached?
  5. Leave a message: a note on the door with the time and destination.

Radio

Radio works without a mobile network, but only over short range. It is a supplement, not a replacement for the emergency call. The range depends strongly on the terrain. In cities and forests often only a few hundred metres up to a few kilometres. That is an estimate.

Radio typeUsable without an exam?Important
PMR446Yes446.0 to 446.2 MHz, 16 channels, at most 0.5 watts radiated power. One transmission may last at most 180 seconds
FreenetYesGermany only. 149 MHz band with six channels
CB radioYes11-metre band at 27 MHz, 80 channels, up to 4 watts radiated power in FM. Channel 9 is considered an emergency channel among CB users – whether anyone is listening is not certain
Amateur radioNo, exam requiredLicense via the Bundesnetzagentur (Federal Network Agency). Beginner class N since 2024
Meshtastic (LoRa)License-free in the SRD band, but observe the rulesShort messages, no mobile network needed. In the 868 MHz range. Check the exact requirements (region, transmission time) with the Bundesnetzagentur

Rules:

  • Use only approved devices with the required marking (according to Wikipedia, the “E” marking) and frequencies permitted for Germany. Do not modify them.
  • Operating devices with more power or on other frequencies is an administrative offence.
  • Radio of the authorities and rescue services (BOS) is not for you.
  • In amateur radio, emergencies and disasters may be exceptions, for example messages for third parties. You still need the license.

Meshtastic connects small LoRa devices into a “mesh”: every device passes messages on. You pair the devices with your phone via Bluetooth. Everyone in the group needs the same settings and the same channel name with key. Set this up beforehand and test it. The messages are short.

Radio talk rules

  • Listen first, then speak. Check whether the channel is free.
  • Agree the channel beforehand: one main channel, one fallback channel.
  • Short and clear: “Anna calling Bernd, Anna calling Bernd, over.” (“Over” means: I have finished, please reply.)
  • Ask for a repeat if you did not understand something. “Out” ends the conversation.
  • Important things first: Where? What? How many?
  • Others listen in: Everyone within range hears. Do not give addresses, names or plans that not everyone should know.
  • Save battery: Take breaks between transmissions, turn down volume and display.

Signalling

If you need help, give conspicuous, regular signals (see also Navigation):

  • Whistle: Three whistles or six per minute (Alpine distress signal), then pause and repeat. A whistle carries further than the voice.
  • Light: Torch or phone light. SOS is three short, three long, three short. See SOS light and Morse code.
  • Mirror: In sunshine, a mirror or shiny object is a very good signalling device. Move it slowly and steadily, not frantically.
  • Colour: Conspicuous clothing, emergency blanket, cloth at the window or on a stick.

On a sunny day, a mirror is your best signaling device.

Saving phone battery

  • Switch on power-saving mode. Turn brightness down.
  • Airplane mode if you have no reception: searching for a network uses a lot of battery. Switch the device on briefly at fixed times (for example every hour). Note: in airplane mode no warnings reach you either.
  • Switch off unnecessary services: location, Bluetooth, background apps.
  • Cold shortens battery life. Carry the phone close to your body.
  • Calls and text messages need less energy than videos and web pages.
  • Keep the power bank charged. The BBK and the Verbraucherzentrale (consumer advice centre) also recommend this. More on this: Power & energy.
