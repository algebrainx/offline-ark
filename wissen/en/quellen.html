How we handle sources

  • Our own texts. All knowledge pages are written in our own words. They summarise recommendations from official bodies and recognised organisations. The sources are listed at the bottom of every page.
  • License checked. Third-party texts are only copied word for word if the license allows it (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, free open-source license). Everything else (e.g. BBK (Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Assistance), DRK (German Red Cross), Umweltbundesamt (Federal Environment Agency)) is only linked and summarised in our own words.
  • Caution first. For medicine, water treatment and plants we use only official or medically recognised sources. If statements contradict each other, the more cautious one applies.
  • No invented links. Every link was checked when it was added. The script scripts/check-links.js checks them again as soon as internet is available. Links only open with internet; everything else here works offline.

Library (PDFs)

The PDFs in the library are public-domain US government works or marked as public domain. They are unchanged and in English.

The two starting sources

  • ligi/SurvivalManual (GitHub, GPL-3.0). An app based on the US Army manual FM 3-05.70. We did not copy the texts from the repository, because the GPL-3.0 does not fit a site as a whole that is licensed under the MIT license. The underlying manual is a US government work; however, for the 2002 edition the PDF carries a distribution notice, which is why it is not in the library here.
  • PR0M3TH3AN/Survival-Data (GitHub). The repository has no license file. Without a license, content may not be reused. We copied nothing.

Wikipedia, Wikibooks, Wikivoyage

Content is mostly under CC BY-SA. We do not copy texts word for word; we only name Wikipedia as a source for facts that we restate in our own words.

Our own code

The HTML, CSS and JavaScript of this project are under the MIT license (see the file LICENSE). The icons are drawn by ourselves. No fonts, libraries or services are loaded from outside.
