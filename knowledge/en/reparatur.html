Repair basics

Shut off the water, leave the building if you smell gas, do not do electrical work yourself – and fix your bicycle. Safety comes before repair.

Does not replace training. In an emergency, call 112 first, or keep trying. This page deliberately gives no medication doses – ask a doctor or pharmacist.

Safety first
  1. Smell of gas: No light switch, no flame, no lighter. Open windows, leave the building, and call 112 or the gas supplier's emergency service from outside.
  2. Water is running out: Turn off the main stopcock. Only then look for the cause.
  3. Electricity: Never work on the house wiring without a qualified electrician. After an electric shock, switch off the power first and do not touch the person.

Water is running out of control

  1. Turn off the main stopcock. It is usually in the cellar or the utility room where the water enters the house. In front of and behind the water meter there is normally a shut-off valve each.
  2. Switch off the power in the wet area if water reaches sockets or the fuse box.
  3. Catch the water: buckets, towels, a mop. Move valuables up.
  4. Call a professional (plumber, property manager, landlord). Take photos for the insurance.

Spotting a slow leak: Turn off all taps. If the water meter keeps running anyway, there is a leak. It can suddenly turn into a burst pipe.

Sealing a pipe: Professionals seal burst pipes with sealing clamps. A clamp or repair tape from a specialist shop can cover a small leak for a while. This is only an emergency fix. Afterwards a professional must repair the pipe.

Practise beforehand

Find the main stopcock today and turn it off and on once. A valve that has never been moved can be stiff. Everyone in the household should know where it is.

Frost: protecting pipes

Water expands when it freezes. Ice can burst pipes. The water often only runs out when the pipe thaws, because the plug of ice closes the hole until then. Keep the main stopcock within reach.

  • Heat the building. Too little heating during frost is a known cause of water damage. If the heating fails: keep one room warm, see Fire, warmth & shelter.
  • Insulate pipes that run outdoors or through cold rooms. Metal pipes are at greater risk than plastic ones.
  • Draw water regularly. Water flowing in is warmer than frosty air. With good insulation this helps against freezing.
  • Draining pipes: This is a recognised protection only for pipes that do not carry drinking water. For drinking-water pipes the source advises against it, because standing water becomes contaminated with germs. If in doubt, ask a plumber.

Smell of gas

Natural gas has hardly any smell of its own. Suppliers add a warning smell, often like rotten eggs. With a leak in the ground this smell is not reliable.

Even a small spark can ignite a gas-air mixture. Light switches and torches can also cause sparks.

  1. Do not switch anything: leave lights, doorbells and plugs alone. No lighter, no cigarette.
  2. Open windows and doors.
  3. Get everyone out of the building, and warn the neighbours (knock instead of ringing the bell).
  4. Call 112 from outside or the gas emergency service.
  5. Never work on gas pipes or gas appliances yourself. Only a professional firm may do that.

Electrics: safety only

Causes of electrical accidents include water on live parts, faulty wiring, repairing appliances yourself, cables without insulation and damaged plugs.

A fuse or RCD has tripped

The circuit breaker (miniature circuit breaker) switches off on overload or short circuit. It does not switch back on by itself.

  1. Unplug the appliances on the affected circuit.
  2. Switch the breaker back on. Circuit breakers are in the fuse box.
  3. If it trips again straight away: do not try again. Call a qualified electrician.
  4. You test the RCD (residual current device) with the test button (T). It only shows that the switch itself works, not that the circuit is in order. The RCD does not prevent an electric shock, it only shortens its duration.

These rules always apply

  • Never work on the house wiring without a qualified electrician. Professionals follow the five safety rules (isolate, secure against reconnection, verify that the system is dead, and more). A sticky note on the fuse box is not enough in a household.
  • Do not keep using damaged cables and plugs. Do not open appliances yourself. Have them replaced or repaired.
  • No back-feeding: A generator may only be connected to the house network through a properly installed changeover switch. See Power & energy off-grid.
Person in contact with electricity
  1. Protect yourself first. Do not touch the person while they are live.
  2. Switch off the power (switch off the breaker, pull the plug). Warn others.
  3. If that is not possible: only use dry, non-conductive objects such as a long plastic broom handle. With high voltage keep your distance and wait for the fire brigade.
  4. Call 112. If the person is unconscious, check breathing and start resuscitation, see First aid.
  5. Even someone who feels well should be examined by a doctor. An electric accident can damage the heart.

Bicycle

Fixing a flat tyre

Bicycle tyres are sensitive to thorns and glass splinters. Take a pump and a repair kit with you. A spare inner tube saves time.

  1. Remove the wheel and lift the tyre off the rim. To do this, push the tyre bead all the way round into the deep centre of the rim. Then tyre levers help.
  2. Feel the inside of the tyre with your fingers. A splinter that stayed behind will puncture the next tube.
  3. Find the hole: Pump up the tube. Hold it to your ear, to your moist lips or under water.
  4. Roughen the spot with sandpaper until it is matt.
  5. Spread a thin layer of vulcanising fluid. Let it air for a few minutes until it no longer feels sticky.
  6. Press the patch on, hard and for a long time. Full adhesion only comes after a few hours. Self-adhesive patches need no fluid.
  7. Put the tyre back on, pump it up, check the valve.

Tyre pressure: Wide tyres run at lower pressure than narrow ones (balloon tyres about 2 bar). Never pump beyond the value on the tyre.

Chain, brakes, light

  • Chain: Lubricate it thinly with oil, at the latest when it squeaks, or after about 100 km or a ride in the rain. Too much oil only binds dirt. A quick link can be opened and closed with pliers. If the chain breaks, the coaster brake no longer works.
  • Brakes: Test them before every ride. With rim brakes the pads wear down. You adjust the brake cable at the threaded sleeve on the brake lever. In the wet, rim brakes work less well, especially on chromed steel rims. Brake earlier and more gently.
  • Light: At dusk and in the dark, lights are mandatory. A battery or rechargeable battery is allowed. Headlight and rear light may be removable, but must then be fitted. Reflectors make you visible in the headlights of others.

Basic toolkit

For the bicycle: multi-tool, three tyre levers, sandpaper, patches, vulcanising fluid, pump, spare inner tube.

For the house (suggestion, not a standard):

  • Hammer, pliers (combination pliers and water-pump pliers), screwdrivers
  • Torch with spare batteries
  • Bucket, rags, towels for water damage
  • Duct tape and cable ties
  • Work gloves and safety glasses

Not included: tools for gas or electrical installations. That is for professionals only.
